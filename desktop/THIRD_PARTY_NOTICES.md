# Third-party notices, ModularCV Desktop 0.5.0-beta.1

ModularCV Desktop is released under the MIT licence (see `LICENSE`, installed next to this file). The installer also carries
third-party components. This file is their **inventory**: it names each component, its version, its licence and where to get
its source. The **licence texts and copyright notices themselves** are in `THIRD_PARTY_LICENSES.txt`, installed in the same
`legal` folder, so everything can be read offline. That file is generated by `desktop/tools/build_third_party_licenses.py` from
the official texts kept in `desktop/licenses/texts/` (each with its source and SHA-256) and from the licence files inside the
published packages of the Rust crates actually compiled in. The inventory below comes from the build's own dependency data
(`cargo tree`, `package-lock.json`, the PyInstaller bundle and the version resources of the shipped DLLs).

No component is licensed under the GPL or LGPL terms alone. Where a component offers a choice of licences ("A OR B"), the
permissive option relied on is stated in `THIRD_PARTY_LICENSES.txt` (order of preference: MIT, Zlib, BSD, 0BSD, MIT-0,
Unlicense, CC0, Apache-2.0, MPL-2.0, Unicode-3.0). Every licence file shipped in a component's own package is reproduced
anyway. This is a best-effort audit, not legal advice; the open items are listed at the end of `THIRD_PARTY_LICENSES.txt`
and in the section "Open items" below.

## Open items

- Microsoft Visual C++ and Universal C runtime files: Microsoft limits redistribution of individual Visual C++ runtime binaries to licensed Visual
  Studio users under the Microsoft Software License Terms. The shipped `VCRUNTIME140.dll` is the copy installed with python.org Python, not the one of
  the build computer's Visual Studio redistributable folder, and the origin of `ucrtbase.dll` and the `api-ms-win-*.dll` files was not established
  (on Windows 10 and 11 the system UCRT is used and these local copies are ignored). Whether the redistribution is covered is not established.
- Whether statically linking the MSVC runtime into `desktop.exe` needs any further notice was not verified.
- The NSIS licence texts are reproduced and the NSIS source location is stated; whether that fully meets the CPL-1.0 for the LZMA module is a reading
  of the licence, not a legal opinion.
- The version of the Unicode data inside `unicodedata.pyd` and of the zlib copy in the PyInstaller bootloader was not determined.
